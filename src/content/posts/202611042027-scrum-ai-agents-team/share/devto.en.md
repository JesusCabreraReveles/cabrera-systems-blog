> Instead of specifying and reviewing everything by hand, we could work with a Scrum team of AI agents and take the Product Owner role ourselves.

Lately I've been thinking a lot about how I work with artificial intelligence. I like using [Spec Driven Development], but I feel too much time goes into writing the spec and reviewing what the AI does. That makes me question whether we are ready to let go of the direct interaction we have, as engineers and developers, with the code.

## Reusing existing frameworks

One alternative is to lean on design frameworks and software factories that already exist. I imagine a tool with several agents, each one with the specific function of a methodology.

## Scrum with agents

Take Scrum. There would be a Scrum Master, an Architect, a lead developer and several Developers. Our role, as experts or as the people building the product, would be Product Owner. It could also be a group of people.

We would meet with the agents and tell them what we need. As experts, they would ask the relevant questions based on predefined rules.

## The full cycle, ceremonies included

What I find interesting is that it would use the same Scrum methodology. The agents would meet and talk to each other. We could spin up as many developers as we wanted, specialized in a tool or a language. They would hand us a Backlog to review.

We would test it as Product Owners, and they would generate the tasks. That way we would go through the whole cycle.

There would be ceremonies, just as in Scrum. At the end of a Sprint, which would be one full programming cycle, the agents would tell us what was delivered and what complications came up. We would review it together. As Product Owner, you can decide right then what change is needed.

## Estimating the cost in tokens

The advantage that appeals to me most is the scoring of each task. The agents and the Scrum Master could estimate, for example, that a user story or an epic will cost a certain number of tokens on average.

This would be especially useful when making changes. It would let us see the impact of our decisions. It would also keep AI consumption from getting out of hand just because we think it can do everything.

## Still understanding what we build

I think this opens the door to continuing to understand what we build. It also lets us see what a change or a request really means.
